      The six fields, written as code

      The starting checker asks for a title and nothing else. The fix checks six fields: the metric, the baseline, the target, the deadline, what stays with people, and the constraints.

      A metric is a number with a unit and a way to measure it. A capability word with nothing measured, such as "agents deployed", is not one. A target with no baseline beside it means nothing. A target that only restates the baseline, such as "below 38 minutes", is a direction: any cut meets it.

      A constraint changes the design, such as data that must stay in the EU or a spend cap. Each has an owner, and when two collide the brief ranks them and says which one gives. Scope is written down too, so a change to it becomes visible.

      Answer each question, then read why every option is right or wrong. The wrong options are the mistakes engineers make on the job.

      1. A regional retailer with 10,000 support tickets a day tells you the board wants agents on the queue inside one 90-day quarter. The budget is fixed at 30,000 dollars a month. The request names the technology and a launch date but no result the business should see, and the three team leads each describe success differently. What do you ask first?

      1. AWhich agent framework and cloud platform the engineers already know best.
      2. BWhich tickets the support team finds most tedious to answer by hand today.
      3. CWhich result must move, by how much, and what that figure is today.
      4. DWhich executive will sign off the budget before the quarter begins.
      Show the answer

      Answer: C

      • A A framework is a way to build. With 90 days on the clock, choosing it before the goal is agreed spends the time on the wrong question.
      • B Tedious tickets are a scope guess. They say where the work is boring, not what the business wants to change or how it would be measured.
      • C Correct. A measured result with today's figure is what the team can test at the end of the quarter.
      • D A name on a sign-off does not say what the project is for, so it settles who approves and nothing about success.

      2. A brief sets the goal "cut handle time by 30 percent" for a pilot that starts in two weeks. Ops measures handle time from first reply to close and reports 14 minutes, while finance measures from creation to close and reports 26, and neither is written down. The readout is fixed for week 12. What comes before the target is set?

      1. AAgree one definition and one baseline figure, then take the 30 percent from it.
      2. BAverage the two figures to 20 minutes, a baseline both teams can accept.
      3. CUse the ops figure, since the ops team runs the queue and knows the work.
      4. DStart the pilot and read the baseline off the first month of live results.
      Show the answer

      Answer: A

      • A Correct. A target is a change from a baseline, and two definitions give two different changes for the same pilot.
      • B An average of two definitions measures nothing real. Neither team can reproduce 20 minutes from its own data.
      • C Taking one team's number settles the argument by rank, and finance then reads a different result at the readout.
      • D A baseline taken after the change cannot show what the change did, and the pilot has no starting point.

      • C A vendor shortlist is part of how to build. It says nothing about what should get better.
      • D Correct. Without today's figure a target cannot be met or missed, and the readout has nothing to compare against.

      • D Asking after go-live commits the spend first. If finance refuses, the cap is already broken.
